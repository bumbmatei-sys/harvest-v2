## 1. Priorities and way of working

- Communities are parked for a while after the open communities PRs merge. Next product work is social media, then the newsletter, then link in bio, blog articles and the Office tools (mail, calendar, files, docs). Communities, events and profiles resume later.
- **Fewer, feature-sized PRs.** One PR per finished feature slice: the server, its screens, its tests and its review fixes together. A database change or a risky security area may get its own earlier PR. Every PR still gets an independent review by a session that did not write it, and merging to `main` follows the founder's standing approval of 20 Nov ("I'm approving if anything is merge ready"), meaning only a PR with an independent reviewer MERGE-OK and green CI on the exact head. A reviewer MERGE-OK is never itself approval. Applying migrations to any real database, deployment, enabling any switch and any activation always need the founder's separate explicit approval.
- **Division of work.** Codex builds server slices; Claude builders build the screens on the same branch afterwards; a separate fresh session reviews. Codex has no access to the paid UI kit, so prompts name the repos and files to read.
- **UI rules.** Layout and organisation come from the founder's first Harvest version (repository `bumbmatei-sys/harvest-agent`, read-only reference outside this repository: `src/components/admin/social/*` and `src/components/admin-shell/shell-nav.ts` live in that repository) and from the Eden reference screenshots (`docs/prd/screenshots/14-social-scheduler/` in that repository). The style comes from Studio Admin. The components come from the premium Shadcn UI Kit: wherever a kit block fits, use it (the founder paid for it); screens PRs name the exact blocks and record honest provenance ("derived from" only for files really copied, with the real identical-line percentage). Platform logo marks already in the first Harvest version may be reused, subject to each platform's brand guidelines. **Analytics and dashboards (founder, 20 Nov):** every analytics screen and dashboard, for example Home in Operations and Public, social analytics, newsletter analytics, giving overview, CRM and campaign dashboards and any future report, uses the premium kit's proper dashboard blocks (stat cards, charts, tables and the dashboard page layouts; the earlier approved items stat-card12, chart56 and chart60 are the starting point), never hand-built charts or look-alikes. The screens PR names the exact blocks under the kit approval gate. Numbers are always real data with explicit unavailable and partial states, never sample figures shown as results. **Dashboards are animated on entry (founder, 20 Nov):** when a page opens the user sees skeleton loading states, then the cards fade and slide in with a short stagger, charts draw in (the chart library's own animation), and stat numbers count up to their value. Rules: the whole entrance finishes in well under a second; it plays once per page visit and not on background refreshes or polling; it is disabled when the user's system asks for reduced motion (then values simply appear); it never delays access to the data; it must stay smooth on a phone. No new animation dependency is added without its own per-slice approval row: use the chart library's built-in animation, CSS transitions and a small count-up hook first; the `motion` library is approved for the kit's chart and stat blocks only (20 Nov, see the decisions log). Screenshots are sent to the founder for every screen PR (light and dark, desktop and phone), and the builder must look at every screenshot before reporting.
- Prompts for Codex are given inline in a code block or as a markdown file.

## 2. Public mode sidebar (approved by the founder, 20 Nov)

Public mode swaps the sidebar (the mode switcher stays beside the logo). Groups are collapsible and **every group header and every row has its own icon, unique across the whole sidebar and in every mode** (not only the group headers). The exact icon per row is chosen in the screens PR from the kit's icon set (the names below are proposals); it must be unique, readable in light and dark, and consistent in weight. Unbuilt entries stay hidden (existing shell rule).

Top of the Public sidebar, above the groups:

- **Discover** (compass; the founder also likes a bonfire or fireplace, final choice in the screens PR).

Groups:

1. **Social media** (share icon): Planner (calendar), Inbox (messages), Auto-DM (send), Analytics (chart). The Planner has its own segmented tabs Compose, Calendar and Queue. Accounts are managed under **Settings > Social accounts** (canonical placement; the Settings group lives in Operations mode) and from the "+" beside "Post to" in the composer. The route stays under `/social/accounts`, but there is no Social media sidebar row for it. The navigation registry today has separate `composer` and `planner` entries and a `socialAccounts` entry in Public mode (draft #157): the screens PR changes the registry so Compose is a tab inside Planner and Accounts is reached from Settings.
2. **Newsletter** (mail): Campaigns, Audience (lists and subscribers), Sign-up forms, Analytics. Analytics only, no inbox (replies go to the reply-to route and later the Office mailbox).
3. **Website** (globe): Link in bio, Blog (articles), Website builder (later).
4. **Community** (a group of people; the chat bubble was rejected because it only means messages): Communities, Live (parked, hidden until built). Discover is not in this group.
5. **Courses and products** (graduation cap): Courses, Products (free for now), Coaching (later), Coupons (later). Courses and products are sold or given outside a community too, so they are their own group, not part of Community. A community's Classroom and Products tab link to the canonical courses and products and never duplicate them.

Proposed icons (final names chosen in the screens PR, unique across the sidebar): Discover compass; Social media share; Planner calendar-days; Inbox messages-square; Auto-DM send; Analytics line chart; Newsletter mail; Campaigns mail-plus; Audience contact-round; Sign-up forms clipboard-pen; Newsletter analytics a different chart glyph from social analytics; Website globe; Link in bio link; Blog newspaper; Website builder layout-template; Community users-round; Communities heart-handshake; Live radio; Courses and products graduation-cap; Courses book-open; Products package; Coaching calendar-clock; Coupons ticket-percent.

Stays elsewhere (unchanged): Events, forms and questionnaires, CRM, Connect, campaigns, volunteers and Settings live in Operations. Chat, private messages, members and a community's own events tab live inside each community's own rail. The assistant keeps its own mode. A page can open in the right pane (existing shell design).

Navigation is implemented only through the typed registry in `src/lib/navigation.ts` (no second nav system, no per-module shell clone). Route registrations and permissions are part of each feature PR.

### 2b. Operations mode sidebar (approved by the founder, 20 Nov)

Operations is the internal side. **This section is the target structure, not what is shipped:** the registry today has the groups `crm`, `connect`, `office`, `workspace` and `settings`; the screens/shell PR that implements this renames and regroups it (`crm` becomes People, `workspace` becomes Ministry, Files moves from `workspace` to Office, Giving becomes its own group, the `settings` group and its entries move into the pop-up, Home is pinned), with navigation tests. Nothing here reads as already shipped. The same rule applies: collapsible groups, every group header and every row with its own unique icon, unbuilt entries hidden, and the typed registry as the only source. Home (dashboard) is pinned above the groups. Order:

1. **Office** (briefcase): **Mail** (the email inbox; each admin connects their own Gmail or Outlook and only that admin reads it), **Calendar** (Google and Outlook), **Files** (Google Drive and OneDrive; moves here from the workspace group), then later **Docs** (notes app), **Sheets**, **Meetings** and **Web** (see below).
2. **People** (contacts): People, Organizations, Pipelines, Tasks, Imports (the CRM).
3. **Connect** (QR code): Follow-up (queue and cases), QR codes and links, Responses, Forms and questionnaires.
4. **Ministry** (church): Campaigns, Events, Volunteers, Ministry page (the public profile), Livestream (parked, hidden until built). Events lives in Operations (16 Nov decision); a community's own events tab stays inside the community.
5. **Giving** (hand with a coin), its own group: Overview (totals and trends), Donations (gifts recorded or confirmed by the ministry: Harvest does not process card payments, never says "verified" or "payment received" for money it did not receive, money in cents), Fundraising (pledge campaigns with goals; design merged), Pledges, Donors (linked to People in the CRM), Funds (designated funds such as missions or building), Giving page (the public page with the ministry's own payment links), Statements (annual giving statements and receipts, later) and Finances (basic income and expenses, later). Dodo stays SaaS billing only; commerce and donation providers are undecided.

**Settings is NOT in the sidebar** (founder, 20 Nov). It is a pop-up opened from the profile menu at the bottom of the sidebar, like a normal SaaS product, with a left list of sections and scrollable content (full screen on phones). Sections: **You** (Profile with name, photo and language; Notifications; Appearance with light and dark; Security and sessions) and **Ministry** (Ministry details; Branding; Team and roles; **Connections**, one place for Gmail and Outlook, calendars, Drive and OneDrive and the social accounts; CRM with fields and pipelines; Privacy and data; Billing later). CRM fields, Pipeline settings, Appearance, Ministry settings and Social accounts therefore leave the sidebar. Mail, Calendar and Files also show a "Connect" button in their empty state. The UI source is chosen from the premium kit (candidates found in the founder's kit repositories: the chat app's `settings-dialog.tsx` as the pop-up shell and the admin dashboard's settings pages for content layouts); the founder sees renderings before anything is built, and the exact kit items still go through the kit approval gate (a list of exact items is approved by the founder before building).

#### Later additions to Office (founder, 20 Nov)

- **Meetings:** a video-meeting experience like Zoom inside Harvest, with AI recording that produces a transcript and notes (saved into Docs, summarised by the assistant). Recording is off by default and visibly indicated to everyone for as long as it runs; consent rules differ by jurisdiction (some require every party's consent), so the design must handle that. Needs its own design and privacy review before building: choice of a meeting provider behind a Harvest-owned port (no in-house video stack), explicit recording consent and a visible notice to every participant, who may start a recording, storage, retention and erasure of recordings and transcripts, minors and safeguarding, and the AI-processing agreement. Hidden until built.
- **Web (embed any website):** a page type that embeds an external website in an iframe inside Harvest, for example the church's own website or any other site. Design points: https only, an allow-list of embedded addresses per ministry managed by owners and admins, a restrictive iframe `sandbox`, no Harvest credentials or cookies ever passed to the embedded site, honest handling of sites that forbid framing (`X-Frame-Options` or `frame-ancestors`): show an "open in a new tab" fallback instead of a blank frame, and permissions for who sees each embed. The allow-list is enforced on the server in the page's Content-Security-Policy `frame-src` (not only in the UI), the frame uses `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` and a `sandbox` without `allow-same-origin` or `allow-top-navigation`, and the feature can never be used to embed the Harvest app itself. Hidden until built.


## 3. Discover

Discover is a **marketplace**, like Skool's Discover, where people find communities and also everything on offer from **every** ministry and creator on Harvest: communities, courses, digital products, coaching and anything else sold or given. It is not limited to one ministry's own items.

- Listing is opt-in by the owner and needs platform approval (11 Nov decision, `docs/prd/harvest-communities.md`); the public page is `/discover`.
- In the sidebar, the Discover row opens the ministry's own view: which of its communities, courses, products (and later coaching) are listed, their approval state, and a link to browse the public Discover page as a visitor sees it.
- Discover shows items from many ministries, so ranking, privacy, moderation and abuse controls are part of its design. Never leak unlisted or draft items, and never expose another ministry's private data. Discover does not duplicate the canonical items: it is a read projection.
- Recommendations (C20) come later.

## 4. Coaching (later)

Needs its own short design before building: availability and time zones, slot booking and cancellation, calendar integration through the provider layer, reminders, what happens while everything is free (the LLC and selling come later), and how it appears in Discover. Hidden in the sidebar until built.

## 5. Social media (three feature PRs, then more)

Scope target: every platform the provider supports, all media formats and all DM features, always subject to what the provider supports per platform and to the existing activation gates. Unsupported features are shown as unavailable, never hidden or faked. All live switches stay hard-off; fake provider and fake accounts in non-production only; no real posting until the founder approves a real provider connection (agreement, test account, OAuth ownership design, key model, quotas, webhook authentication, scanner and legal items).

### 5.1 PR 1: Publish (server in the Publish PR, screens on the same branch)

- **Platforms:** the provider documents 16 posting destinations (X, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, Reddit, Bluesky, Threads, Google Business Profile, Telegram, Snapchat, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack); Snapchat (closed beta) and WhatsApp recipient messaging are unavailable for ordinary Publish drafts; blogs and messaging-only connections are mapped in the acceptance record (25 platform keys in total).
- **Composer (single page, no stepper sidebar):** top bar with schedule switcher (default and per-platform schedules), Create / Queue / Calendar tabs, Drafts count, Save draft, a split Schedule button (time or next free slot), Publish now with a review step. Left card: "Post to" account circles, one text card with the media strip inside it and attach and emoji controls, then option rows with switches (first comment, auto-repost, made with AI, link, TikTok privacy and explicit consent). Right column: preview with platform tabs (All, then each platform) and an "Each platform" list where each row shows "Same as all" and the character count and opens a per-platform override (custom text, which media the platform accepts, reel or standard format).
- **Progress rings (founder requirement):** each connected account's logo is a ring around it that fills as the user types, showing how much of that platform's character limit is used (a warning colour near and over the limit, and the number is always shown too). Hovering (and focusing or tapping) a logo opens a popup with the platform name, the total limit and the characters written, for example "About 140 / 280", using a premium kit hover card. All counts come from one shared per-platform counting function used by the server and the screens; counts the server marks as estimates are labelled as estimates.
- **Media (founder requirement):** images, multi-image carousels (reorder, remove, alt text per item, per-platform item limits, mixed image and video where a platform allows it), GIFs, videos, reels and shorts, stories and other allowed attachments, with drag-and-drop, a picker, large-file multipart upload with progress and cancel, thumbnails, a video cover-frame picker, per-destination accepted or rejected reasons from a versioned per-platform media capability table, and a reel switch per destination. Never silently convert a video into an image post. Each attached image card keeps its four controls (move left, move right, edit, remove) in one compact row so the card stays short (founder request), with 44px tap targets and labels.
- **Media safety (conservative numbers, founder decision):** the temporary download link given to the provider lives 2 hours by default (never longer than the provider's documented fetch window), is minted fresh for every attempt and retry, and a provider fetch failure shows a clear recoverable state; abandoned or incomplete uploads are cleaned up after 12 hours; at most 5 unfinished uploads per ministry; 4 GB per file by default, lowered per platform; a 25 GB soft limit of stored social media per ministry with a warning at 80 percent (to become a plan limit later). The cleanup job is hard-off outside non-production.
- **Planner:** header "Planner" with tabs Compose, Calendar, Queue. Queue = three columns, Drafts, Scheduled (grouped by day) and Sent, with per-destination status and retry, reschedule by a date and time field, duplicate and cancel (the UI never claims everything was cancelled when a delivery is already sending or sent). Calendar = week grid with a drafts panel plus a month toggle; Today, previous and next, "+ New post"; move a post by dragging or by a date and time field, with a version check and confirmation.
- **Publishing rules:** Harvest owns scheduling (the worker asks the provider to publish now); partial success is shown per destination; retry only eligible failed destinations with the original idempotency key; an ambiguous outcome stays "unknown" until reconciled; the publish-now review token binds destinations and content; auto-repost keeps the previous remote copy and **Harvest never deletes a remote post automatically**.
- **Accounts:** under Settings > Social accounts and from the "+" beside "Post to"; cards with health and capabilities and an explicit unavailable state (never an empty list). Real account connection needs a reviewed OAuth ownership design and the provider's own sign-in; connecting uses the fake provider in non-production for now. Never ask users to paste provider secrets.
- **Blog articles (later, own feature):** the composer shows an Article tab that says "connect a blog to use this". The contract (founder, 20 Nov): full Markdown source with all formatting, links, YouTube embeds and the blocks a blog editor such as WordPress offers; converted by the server per blog (proposed default: Markdown stays the source in Harvest and a preview shows unsupported elements); sent as draft, scheduled or live; later changes (proposed default): Harvest keeps its own copy, an edit reaches the blog only by an explicit update, deleting in Harvest never deletes the remote article, and Harvest marks the article as changed if the blog's copy differs; a blog is connected through the provider's sign-in flow; targets WordPress, Shopify, Wix and Webflow.

### 5.2 PR 2: One unified inbox with all DM features

- One inbox for every connected account and platform: DMs, comments, mentions and story replies, with the Studio Admin mail-app sidebar organisation (folders All, Unread, Assigned to me, Open, Done, Starred, Mentions, Comments, DMs; platform and account channel groups with counts; labels). The email inbox is separate (Operations, per-admin mailbox); newsletter has no inbox; community chat is separate.
- Message and comment text is live pass-through and never stored. Harvest-local metadata only: read in Harvest, assigned, status, starred, labels, snooze, internal notes (staff-authored), saved replies with variables, working hours and away message. Local markers never claim a platform mailbox was marked read.
- Human replies through a separately reviewed worker job (eligibility, provider window, idempotency, ambiguous-outcome reconciliation; text, media, comment reply, comment-to-DM private reply, quick replies). Link an inbox item to a CRM contact or create a follow-up task only by explicit user action; an inbound message creates no contact and grants no consent. Bulk actions, search on local fields, polling, capability labels per platform and an expired connection that never looks like an empty inbox.
- Reply rules (founder, as recommended): a human reply counts as a service response only when it answers a message the person sent, inside the platform's standard reply window, with no promotional content; promotional messages go through the separate consent rules. Do not use Meta's extended human-agent window for now.
- Known pre-activation items: the reply body handoff must work for a standalone worker; hashed short replies need a key policy; internal notes may hold pasted message text and must be covered by retention and erasure docs.

### 5.3 PR 3: Auto-DM (Instagram and Facebook only)

Versioned rules, disabled by default, with an explicit enable review; triggers only where the provider truly supports them (comment keyword on a post or any post, DM keyword, story reply, story mention, welcome message, ice-breaker or menu entries); match policy, response with variables, optional public comment reply, media, quick replies, cooldown and daily limits per recipient and per rule, working hours and away message, stop conditions and hand-off to a human; test mode on synthetic recipients; metadata-only logs and counts; dispatch-time re-checks of consent, window, eligibility and limits; withdrawal cancels queued work. Known limits: live comment triggers are unavailable until a verifiable live lookup exists; the welcome trigger depends on observable message history; a withdrawal in the instant between the last check and the send cannot be recalled. Service-only templates: the ministry owner or an admin approves each template once as service-only (an explicit confirmation that it only answers what the person asked and contains no offers, sales or fundraising asks, recorded in the audit log with the exact version), Harvest flags likely promotional wording automatically, and legal review of this policy is a gate before any activation (founder, as recommended).

### 5.4 PR 4: Social analytics

Numeric metric snapshots only (no post text or participant data), with retention; date-range summary, time series, top and bottom posts, per-platform breakdown, best times to post (day and hour heatmap), per-post detail linked to the planner record; measured zero is distinguished from unavailable; partial results for delayed or expired accounts; owner and admin only. Screens follow the first Harvest version's Analytics tab built with kit blocks (stat cards, charts).
